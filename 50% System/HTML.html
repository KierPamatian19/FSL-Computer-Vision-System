Why communication access matters

Communication is an essential part of human life. It lets people express thoughts, needs, emotions, and ideas. For Deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals, sign language is a primary means of communication.

In the Philippines, Filipino Sign Language (FSL) is the language of the Filipino Deaf community. It is recognized as the national sign language under the FSL Act (Republic Act No. 11106).

According to Montefalco (2022), the Filipino deaf community continues to lag behind a fast-paced, technology-driven society. FSL has contributed to better communication for deaf people. However, the majority of the population in the Philippines does not understand FSL.

How the system works

This study develops and evaluates a computer vision-based system for recognizing FSL gestures. Select a stage to see what happens at each step.

Try it live

Teach the page a gesture, then sign it again and watch it get recognized. Hand tracking runs in your browser, and your camera video is never uploaded.

  1. Start the camera. Allow access and wait for the hand model to load.
  2. Teach a gesture. Type a name, press Record, and hold the sign steady for a few seconds.
  3. Sign it to recognize it. Hold a taught gesture and it is added to the transcript.
